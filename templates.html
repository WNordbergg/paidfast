1 · Friendly nudge (+3–7 days)

Subject: Friendly reminder: Invoice #1042 ($2,400)

Hi [client],

Hope you're doing well! Just a quick, friendly note that invoice #1042
for $2,400, due on August 7, is showing as unpaid in our system.

If payment is already on its way, feel free to ignore this — but a quick
confirmation of when we can expect it would be much appreciated.

If anything needs correcting or you're missing paperwork, let me know
and I'll sort it right away.

Thanks so much!

Best,
[Your name]

2 · Firm reminder (+14 days)

Subject: Overdue: Invoice #1042 ($2,400) — 14 days past due

Hi [client],

Invoice #1042 for $2,400 was due on August 7 and is now 14 days
overdue. We haven't received payment or an update on when to expect it.

Please arrange payment within the next 5 business days, or reply to
this email with the exact date payment will be sent.

If something about the invoice is blocking payment, raise it today and
we'll resolve it immediately.

Regards,
[Your name]

3 · Final notice (+30 days)

Subject: FINAL NOTICE — Invoice #1042 ($2,400), 30 days overdue

Hi [client],

Despite earlier reminders, invoice #1042 for $2,400, due on August 7,
remains unpaid — now 30 days past the due date.

This is a formal demand for payment. Full payment must be received
within 7 days of this notice (by September 15). If it is not:

  • late-payment interest will be charged from the original due date,
    plus recovery costs,
  • the matter will be referred to a collections agency,
  • and any ongoing work will be paused until the account is settled.

If payment has already been sent, reply with the transfer reference and
we'll stand down immediately.

Rules that make reminders actually get paid

  • One clear action. Every email should ask for exactly one thing: pay, or reply with a date.
  • Never apologize for asking. You delivered work. The money is yours; reminders are normal business.
  • Make it easy to pay. Repeat the amount, reference, and payment link/details in every message.
  • Stay boring. No anger, no sarcasm. Firmness comes from deadlines and consequences, not adjectives.
  • Put it in writing. Follow calls with an email summary — it builds the paper trail you need if it escalates.
  • Late fees: EU directive 2011/7/EU entitles businesses to interest + recovery costs on commercial late payments; Sweden adds dröjsmålsränta by law; in the US, check your contract and state law. Even just mentioning legitimate fees motivates payment.
